# Strategy

- Rubric: quality 60% (weighted rating of the valid answers over each request's best legal free rating), served 15%, wire 10% (shape + streaming checks), failover 10%, catalog 5%; tie-break = fewer provider calls, then earlier receipt.
- Objective is lexicographic: score 99 first, then fewest provider calls. With served/wire/failover/catalog at 100 the score is round((60*quality + 400)/100): quality 98 gives 98.8 and quality 99 gives 99.4, both round to 99 - the quality column may give up its top point down to the floor 100*earned >= 98*bound, i.e. up to 2% of the trial's quality points, when that saves upstream calls. Quality above 98 never buys score - the router never spends a call to raise 98 to 99.
- Dev board state: 3,992 entries tied at score 99 (the cap: 0.6*99 quality + 40 = 99.4), 119 of them at the (99, 20-calls) optimum; tie-break = fewer calls then EARLIER receipt, so the dev podium is structurally locked to earlier entries. The win condition is the FINAL on hidden instances, where only entries that reproduce (99, ~20) there rank. Quality 100 is unreachable on any instance (17 of 20 requests peak on one provider whose free-tier budget and refusal schedule can never cover them), so the real contest is score 99 - any weighted quality >= 98 - with the fewest upstream calls.
- Generalization guarantees: the catalog is probed from each provider's own route in its own auth scheme (never the brief alone), so it matches whatever the final instance lists; capabilities, ratings and the per-request quality bound come from the brief; nothing is hardcoded from the practice trials. The hidden per-trial inputs (budgets, refusal schedules, the outage, the designated second-call refusal) cannot be derived at trial time - the harness hands the entry a stand-in trial seed, not the instance seed - so the router runs on priors plus observation instead: each provider's refusal risk is modeled from the sealed tier bounds (fail numbers land in [2, budget-1], so a first call is always safe while depth on a narrow-window provider is risky; one provider of ten refuses its second call; one goes out mid-trial; a refused call still consumes its call number).
- Planner: before every attempt the remaining requests are matched to provider call slots by a min-cost-flow (successive shortest paths over the small request x provider graph): unit edges carry the weighted quality loss of the provider's best legal model, slot edges carry scaled refusal risk priced at the worst risk seen so far on that provider (so the solver consumes call numbers strictly in order). mu-escalation multiplies the loss weight until the plan fits the quality-98 budget minus the loss already sunk; a few steeply surcharged emergency slots past the planned depth let that escalation buy quality back when refusals have eaten the planned capacity. Every refusal re-plans from the observed state ("budget is spent" / "is out" kill a provider; "refused this call" reveals one scheduled fail number and puts a surcharge on that provider's deeper numbers); determinism unchanged: no wall clock, no unseeded randomness, one provider call at a time, manifest order - the trial replays byte for byte.
- Wires: requests are translated between OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini wires (system, tools + tool_choice, images, JSON mode - only onto wires that carry it, max_tokens, stops, stream) so the provider's echo matches the client byte for byte; provider calls are made non-streaming and client SSE (event order, [DONE]/message_stop, usage placement, stop-reason families) is synthesized from the provider's answer with its exact usage counts.
- Run it: `node main.ts` inside a trial (Node 22, stdlib only); `node main.ts --list` prints the catalog document byte for byte as registered, `{"providers":[]}` with no brief.
